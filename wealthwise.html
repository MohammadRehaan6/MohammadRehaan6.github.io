WealthWise Start

A gamified financial-literacy platform that teaches students how to spend and save through interactive lessons and games, not lectures.

Category

EdTech · gamification

Audience

College students

My role

Solo build

The problem

Most students reach college having never been taught the basics of managing money — budgeting, saving, avoiding debt — and traditional financial-literacy content (long articles, lecture-style courses) rarely holds attention long enough to actually change behavior.

The approach

WealthWise Start reframes financial literacy as something closer to a game you keep coming back to than a course you finish once. Students work through short interactive lessons on budgeting and saving, then reinforce what they've learned through games, quizzes, and tasks — earning points, badges, and a daily streak along the way.

A persistent dashboard keeps progress visible at all times: current level, points balance, streak length, badges earned, and an overall completion percentage — so the incentive to come back tomorrow is always in view, not buried in a menu.

What it does

  • Interactive lessons on budgeting, saving, and managing money as a student
  • Games, quizzes, and short tasks that test and reinforce financial concepts
  • A reward system — levels, points, streaks, and badges — that keeps engagement up over time
  • A single dashboard tracking overall progress across lessons and activities

Why it matters

The core idea is that financial habits are built through repetition, not a single class. Making the learning loop feel like a game — rather than a chore — is what makes students likely to actually return day after day, which is where real behavior change happens.
